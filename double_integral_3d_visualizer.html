1 Base Region $R$ & Area Element $dA$

We partition the rectangular domain $R = [a, b] \times [c, d]$ on the $xy$-plane into $m \times n$ tiny sub-rectangles. Each grid cell has width $\Delta x = \frac{b-a}{m}$, depth $\Delta y = \frac{d-c}{n}$, and area:

$$\Delta A = \Delta x \cdot \Delta y \;\longrightarrow\; dA = dx\,dy$$
2 Height $z = f(x_i^*, y_j^*)$

Inside each sub-rectangle $R_{ij}$, we pick a sample point $(x_i^*, y_j^*)$ and evaluate the surface height $z = f(x_i^*, y_j^*)$ directly above the $xy$-plane.

3 Volume of One Prism ($dV$)

A thin rectangular column (shown in gold) standing on base $\Delta A$ with height $f(x_i^*, y_j^*)$ has volume:

$$\Delta V_{ij} = f(x_i^*, y_j^*) \, \Delta x \, \Delta y \;\longrightarrow\; dV = f(x,y)\,dA$$
4 Summing & Taking the Limit

Adding all $m \times n$ prisms approximates the total volume. As $m, n \to \infty$, the stepped columns merge into the exact smooth volume under $z = f(x, y)$:

$$V = \lim_{m,n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n} f(x_i^*, y_j^*)\,\Delta A = \iint_R f(x,y)\,dA$$
